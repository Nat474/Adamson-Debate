## I. PROJECT DESCRIPTION

The Adamson Debate Society Training Program is a comprehensive, structured educational initiative designed to develop students' critical thinking, argumentation, research, and public speaking skills through systematic debate training. The program operates on British Parliamentary (BP) and Asian Parliamentary (AP) debate formats, offering three membership tiers (Resident, Varsity, Officer) and providing progressive skill development from foundational concepts to advanced competitive strategies.

### Core Components

**Training Structure:**
- 12-week BP Debate Training Sprint (October 2025 - January 2026)
- Weekly 3-hour training sessions (2:00 PM - 5:00 PM)
- Beginner and Advanced tracks for differentiated learning
- Tournament simulation and panel adjudication

**Resource Infrastructure:**
- Digital resource library with templates, theory materials, and analytics tools
- Motion banks and briefing worksheets
- Judge feedback systems and performance tracking
- Case construction toolkits and argumentation playbooks

**Technology Platform:**
- Supabase-integrated website for content management
- Dynamic news and events management system
- Resource categorization (Community, Training, Tournaments)
- Member engagement tracking and analytics

**Membership Structure:**
- **Resident Members:** ₱50 fee, access to all activities and training
- **Varsity Members:** Free membership, competitive tournament participation
- **Officer Members:** Executive leadership roles through election

### Mission & Vision Alignment

**Mission:**
The Adamson University Debate Society upholds core principles that guide our mission as an official student organization:
- Molding members in accordance with DELIVERS core values
- Valuing diversity of opinions, backgrounds, and experiences
- Upholding freedom of expression and academic freedom
- Promoting fairness and equality of opportunity

**Vision:**
Strategic objectives include:

## V. PROGRAM FLOW

### Phase 1: Recruitment & Onboarding (August - September 2025)

**Week 1-2: Pre-Launch Preparation**
- Finalize training curriculum and resource materials

- Recruit and brief training facilitators and coaches
- Prepare membership application systems and databases

**Week 3-4: SOAR Recruitment Drive**
- **Activities:**
  - Booth setup at Student Organization Activity Registration (SOAR)
  - Organizational showcase with demonstration debates
  - Information sessions explaining membership tiers and benefits
  - One-on-one conversations with prospective members
  - Distribution of promotional materials and application forms

- **Deliverables:**
  - 50+ interested prospective members engaged
  - 30+ completed membership applications submitted
  - Membership database created with contact information
  - Social media following established for ongoing engagement

**Week 5-6: Orientation & Assessment**
- **Activities:**
  - New member orientation session explaining organizational structure
  - Constitution review and membership commitment signing
  - Baseline skills assessment (written and oral components)
  - Team-building activities and icebreaker sessions
  - Introduction to debate formats (BP and AP)

- **Deliverables:**
  - 30+ officially enrolled resident members
  - Baseline assessment data for all members
  - Member profiles created in database system
  - Training cohorts organized by experience level

### Phase 2: Foundational Training (October 2025)

**Week 1: Foundations of BP Debate (October 11, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Speaker roles, argument layering, intro to flowing for opening benches
- **Activities:**
  - Lecture on BP format structure and team dynamics
  - Demonstration debate by varsity members/alumni
  - Workshop on argument construction and evidence use
  - Practice flowing exercises with sample speeches
  - Small group drills on speaker role responsibilities
- **Deliverables:** Members understand basic BP structure and can identify speaker roles

**Week 2: Roles & Case Construction (October 18, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Modeling first half discipline with primer drills on burdens and split logic
- **Activities:**
  - Deep dive into Prime Minister and Leader of Opposition roles
  - Case construction workshop with model cases
  - Burden analysis exercises for different motion types
  - Split logic practice for DPM and DLO positions
  - Mini-debates with structured feedback
- **Deliverables:** Members can construct basic cases and understand argumentation burdens

**Week 3: Argumentation & Rebuttal (October 25, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Refutation ladders, framing answers, and targeted micro-clash rounds
- **Activities:**
  - Rebuttal techniques lecture and demonstration
  - Framing workshop on how to win clash battles
  - Micro-clash drills focusing on specific argument exchanges
  - Point of Information (POI) practice sessions
  - Full-length practice debates with rebuttal focus
- **Deliverables:** Members proficient in direct refutation and responsive argumentation

**Week 4: Advanced Rebuttal & Clash (November 1, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Second half focus on responsive bench strategies and depth weighing
- **Activities:**
  - Extension construction workshop for closing teams
  - Depth weighing exercises comparing argument impacts
  - Strategic positioning practice for Member and Whip speeches
  - Advanced POI strategy and timing drills
  - Full BP round simulation with detailed adjudication
- **Deliverables:** Members understand closing team responsibilities and extension requirements

### Phase 3: Thematic Training (November - December 2025)

**Week 5: Value Frameworks (Ethics) (November 8, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Moral metric development and util/rights balancing through drills
- **Activities:**
  - Philosophy primer on major ethical frameworks
  - Case study analysis of ethical dilemmas
  - Weighing mechanisms workshop (util vs. rights)
  - Debates on morally complex motions
  - Judge feedback on ethical argumentation quality
- **Deliverables:** Members can construct sophisticated moral arguments and weigh ethical impacts

**Week 6: Justice & Cultural Constructs (November 15, 2025)**
- **Topics:** High context motions, narrative nuance, and judge adaptation labs
- **Activities:**
  - Cultural competency training for international motions
  - Case studies on social justice movements
  - Intersectionality and identity politics discussions
  - Debates on culturally sensitive topics
  - Judge adaptation exercises with varied judging philosophies
- **Deliverables:** Members demonstrate cultural awareness and nuanced argumentation

**Week 7: Policy & Governance Systems (November 22, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Analytical frameworks for state actor motions and comparative models
- **Activities:**
  - Policy analysis methodology training
  - Comparative politics briefings
  - Government systems comparison exercises
  - Stakeholder analysis workshops
  - Full rounds on policy implementation motions
- **Deliverables:** Members proficient in policy debate and institutional analysis

**Week 8: Economic Theories in Debate (November 29, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Macro/micro briefings, trade-offs, and economic weighing techniques
- **Activities:**
  - Economics crash course (macro and micro concepts)
  - Trade-off analysis methodology
  - Cost-benefit analysis workshops
  - Market failure and intervention debates
  - Debates on economic policy motions
- **Deliverables:** Members can make sophisticated economic arguments with proper weighing

**Week 9: International Relations & Ideologies (December 6, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Global affairs motions, ideological framing, and actor analysis
- **Activities:**
  - IR theory overview (realism, liberalism, constructivism)
  - Geopolitical analysis training
  - Actor interests and capabilities workshops
  - Debates on foreign policy and global governance
  - Security studies and conflict resolution exercises
- **Deliverables:** Members understand IR frameworks and can analyze global affairs motions

### Phase 4: Strategic Development (December 2025)

**Week 10: Strategy & Extensions (December 13, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Second bench strategy: outflanking, wrapping, and stakeholder extensions
- **Activities:**
  - Strategic positioning workshop for closing teams
  - Extension construction masterclass
  - Outflanking techniques and practice
  - Wrapping the debate exercises
  - Full BP rounds with strategic focus
- **Deliverables:** Members demonstrate advanced strategic thinking and positioning

**Week 11: Whips & Team Dynamics (December 20, 2025)**
- **Topics:** Closing half synergy, whip collapse techniques, and time management
- **Activities:**
  - Whip speech structure and best practices
  - Team coordination and case synergy workshops
  - Collapse strategy training
  - Time management and speech pacing drills
  - Full BP rounds with whip focus and detailed feedback
- **Deliverables:** Members proficient in whip responsibilities and team coordination

### Phase 5: Tournament Simulation & Assessment (January 2026)

**Week 12: Tournament Simulation & Feedback (January 10, 2026)**
- **Topics:** Full BP rounds with panel adjudication, rankings, and development plans
- **Activities:**
  - All-day tournament simulation event
  - Multiple preliminary rounds with diverse motions
  - Panel adjudication with oral feedback
  - Individual performance evaluations and rankings
  - One-on-one development planning sessions
  - Varsity team selection announcements
- **Deliverables:**
  - Comprehensive performance assessments for all members
  - Individual development plans with specific improvement areas
  - Varsity team roster finalized for upcoming tournaments
  - Training program evaluation and feedback collection

  - Implement fundraising strategies and alumni engagement

**Risk 3: Limited Coaching Capacity**
- **Likelihood:** Low | **Impact:** High
- **Mitigation:**
  - Recruit multiple coaches to distribute workload
  - Leverage alumni and experienced varsity members as mentors
  - Partner with external organizations for training support
  - Invest in training-the-trainer programs for sustainability

**Risk 4: Poor Tournament Performance**
- **Likelihood:** Low | **Impact:** Medium
- **Mitigation:**
  - Ensure adequate preparation and practice before tournaments
  - Provide realistic expectations and growth mindset coaching
  - Focus on learning and development rather than only wins
  - Celebrate incremental improvements and effort

**Risk 5: Digital Platform Technical Issues**
- **Likelihood:** Medium | **Impact:** Low
- **Mitigation:**
  - Choose reliable hosting and database solutions (Vercel, Supabase)
  - Implement regular backups and disaster recovery plans
  - Maintain simple, user-friendly design minimizing complexity
  - Have technical support resources available for troubleshooting

## VIII. CONCLUSION

The Adamson Debate Society Training Program represents a comprehensive, evidence-based approach to developing students' critical thinking, communication, and leadership capabilities through structured debate education. By combining rigorous training, competitive opportunities, digital innovation, and community engagement, the program aims to create lasting impact on individual members, the university, and the broader community.

**Program Distinctiveness:**
- **Structured Progression:** 12-week curriculum with differentiated tracks ensures all members develop foundational to advanced skills
- **Competitive Excellence:** Focus on tournament success and speaker development prepares students for national/international competition
- **Digital Integration:** Supabase-powered platform provides modern, accessible resource management and engagement tools
- **Holistic Development:** Emphasis on leadership, ethics, and civic engagement extends beyond technical debate skills

**Sustainability Strategy:**
The program's long-term viability depends on:
- Building strong organizational culture and member loyalty
- Establishing diverse, reliable funding streams
- Developing leadership pipeline ensuring smooth transitions
- Creating valuable partnerships with academic and external stakeholders
- Maintaining flexibility to adapt to changing circumstances and opportunities

**Measurement & Accountability:**
Success will be rigorously evaluated through:
- Quantitative metrics (tournament results, membership numbers, retention rates)
- Qualitative assessments (member satisfaction, skill development, stakeholder feedback)
- Regular program evaluations and continuous improvement cycles
- Transparent reporting to stakeholders on progress and challenges

**Vision for Impact:**
Ultimately, the Adamson Debate Society aspires to be recognized as a premier institution for intellectual development, producing graduates who are:
- Critical thinkers capable of analyzing complex issues from multiple perspectives
- Effective communicators able to articulate ideas clearly and persuasively
- Ethical leaders committed to evidence-based reasoning and constructive dialogue
- Engaged citizens contributing positively to democratic discourse and social progress

This project charter serves as the foundational document guiding the Adamson Debate Society's operations for Academic Year 2025-2026 and beyond, providing clear direction while maintaining flexibility to adapt and innovate in service of our mission and members.
